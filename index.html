Three methods run almost every app

Pick the method first and the app shortlist mostly picks itself.

METHOD 01

Zero-based budgeting

Every dollar of income gets a job before the month starts — bills, debt, savings, fun — until income minus plan equals zero. Demanding, and that's why it works for people who've failed with looser tracking.

YNAB · EveryDollar

METHOD 02

Digital envelopes

The cash-envelope system, without the cash. Each category is an envelope with a hard limit; when the grocery envelope is empty, grocery spending stops. Simple, visual, and great for shared household spending.

Goodbudget · EveryDollar

METHOD 03

Safe-to-spend

The app does the maths — income minus bills, goals and regular spending — and shows one number: what's genuinely left to spend. Lowest effort by far; the trade-off is less control over where money leaks.

PocketGuard · Rocket Money · Simplifi

Questions people actually ask

What happened to Mint?

Intuit shut Mint down in March 2024 and pointed users to Credit Karma, which tracks spending but doesn't really budget — no category budgets or goals. Monarch Money and Quicken Simplifi (both from ex-Mint-adjacent teams) are where most Mint refugees landed, which is why they top so many 2026 lists.

Is a budgeting app worth paying for?

Only if it changes a number that matters. If a $100-a-year app stops one overdraft a month or one forgotten-subscription pile-up, it's paid for itself several times over. If you'll open it twice and drift, the free tiers — Rocket Money, Goodbudget, EveryDollar — are the honest starting point.

Which app is best if I don't want to link my bank account?

Goodbudget. It's built for manual entry — no bank connection required at all — and its envelope system works fine typed in by hand. EveryDollar's free tier is also manual-entry. The trade-off is discipline: miss a week of entries and the budget stops reflecting reality.

What's the best app for couples?

Monarch Money. One subscription covers the household with unlimited collaborators, both partners see the same accounts, budgets and net worth, and neither person has to be "the money person" relaying screenshots. Goodbudget is the budget-friendly runner-up for envelope fans.

Can I use two apps at once?

Plenty of people do, and it can be the smartest setup: Rocket Money's free tier standing guard over subscriptions, plus one "real" budgeting app — YNAB or Monarch — running the actual budget. They solve different problems rather than competing.

Why isn't there one winner?

Because the failure mode is different for everyone. Overspenders need limits (envelopes, zero-based), the disorganized need automation (Monarch, Simplifi), and the subscription-hoarders need an audit (Rocket Money). The best app is the one matched to your actual leak — which is what the finder above is for.
